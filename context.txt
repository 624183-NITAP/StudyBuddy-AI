A I R P O L L U T I O N
Open burning, dust, odour and landfill gas
Air impacts occur at collection points, dumpsites, transfer stations, processing plants and transport corridors.
OPEN BURNING
particulate matter • carbon monoxide • acid gases • toxic organic
pollutants
ODOUR
hydrogen sulphide • ammonia • volatile organic compounds
DUST & BIOAEROSOLS
fine particles • fungal spores • microbial fragments
LANDFILL GAS
methane • carbon dioxide • trace gases • fire risk
Control priorities Ban and prevent open burning
Cover and contain waste during transport
Capture or flare landfill gas
Use ventilation and emission controls at facilities

C L I M A T E I M P A C T S
Methane, combustion and lifecycle emissions
Climate impact is shaped by the organic fraction, disposal conditions, transport and avoided material production.
MAJOR EMISSION PATHWAYS
Anaerobic decay of food and garden waste produces methane.
Open burning releases carbon dioxide, black carbon and other
pollutants.
Collection vehicles and processing facilities consume fuel and
electricity.
Discarding recyclables increases demand for virgin-material
production.
CLIMATE MITIGATION LEVERS
Prevent food waste
Compost or digest clean organics
Capture landfill gas
Recycle high-value materials
Optimize routes and vehicles

S O I L A N D L A N D
Contamination, instability and loss of productive land
Uncontrolled disposal alters soil quality, occupies land and creates long-term liabilities.
Chemical contamination
batteries • oils • pesticides • paint • ash
Plastic fragmentation
microplastics and additives persist in soil
Land degradation
loss of vegetation, amenity and development value
Slope instability
waste slides after rain, gas or poor placement
Windblown litter
plastic and paper disperse beyond the site
Long-term liability
monitoring and remediation continue after closure
Separate hazardous household waste and use engineered liners, cover systems, stable slopes and site restoration.

O C C U P A T I O N A L S A F E T Y
Risks faced by collection workers and informal recyclers
Mixed waste hides sharp, infectious, chemical and combustible hazards.
! Cuts and punctures glass • metal • needles
! Traffic injuries reversing vehicles • roadside work
! Machinery hazards conveyors • compactors • shredders
! Biological exposure food waste • sanitary waste • bioaerosols
! Fire and explosion batteries • gas • hot ash
! Ergonomic strain manual lifting • repetitive handling
Minimum safeguards source segregation • PPE • vaccination and hygiene • machine guards • traffic plans • training • formal integration and social protection

Priority actions for municipalities and institutions
Controls should prevent impacts at source before relying on treatment or cleanup.
1 PREVENT procurement • food-waste reduction • reuse
2 SEGREGATE wet • dry • sanitary • special-care
3 COLLECT reliable service • covered transport • route control
4 RECOVER MRF • compost • anaerobic digestion
5 CONTROL emissions • leachate • gas • fire • worker safety
6 DISPOSE sanitary landfill for rejects and inert waste

K E Y T A K E A W A Y S
Municipal waste impacts are preventable
Use these statements as a revision checklist.
1 Improper MSW management contaminates air, water and soil. 2 Organic waste in uncontrolled disposal generates methane and odour.
3 Litter blocks drains, worsens flooding and creates vector habitat. 4 Workers face sharp, biological, traffic, machinery and fire hazards.
5 Pollution damages ecosystems and may enter food chains. 6 Social and economic costs extend far beyond municipal collection budgets.
7 Source segregation is the foundation of safe recovery and treatment. 8 Engineered controls are necessary even after prevention and recycling.
Clean cities require both good infrastructure and disciplined everyday operations.
